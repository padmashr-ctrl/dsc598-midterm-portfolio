01

Concept

How much of a gesture remains yours after a system learns to continue it?

Signal Garden is a browser-based generative artwork about shared agency. A visitor contributes a line; the system reads its speed, direction, closure and scale, then grows a response that may drift away from the original intention.

The experience begins with direct control and ends with observation. A single slider—system agency—sets how faithful the continuation is. Low, and the system echoes the hand. High, and it treats the gesture as a suggestion. In between, authorship passes back and forth.

It grew out of the course's first two weeks: Conway's Game of Life and Vera Molnar's parameter studies on how simple rules produce unpredictable form, and the cybernetic case studies—Pask, Cohen's AARON, Sougwen Chung—on when a responsive system starts to feel like a collaborator.

02

Four interpretations

The system names its reading on the canvas, so its decisions are never hidden.

01

Orbit

A gesture that nearly closes becomes a rotating memory. New particles keep revisiting its centre.

02

Branch

A vertical gesture becomes a scaffold. The system splits it into smaller, less predictable decisions.

03

Drift

A fast or wide gesture keeps its momentum, then bends toward the surrounding field instead of the hand.

04

Pulse

A compact gesture becomes a signal source, sending rhythmic rings through nearby responses.

03

Same input, three agencies

Live. Each garden receives the identical scripted gestures—orbit, drift, branch—every few seconds. Only the agency differs.

04

Fine-tuning

Changes from the first version to this one.

Issue in v1Change
The field was empty until someone drew, so first-time visitors saw a black screen.It opens with a gesture already growing, and an idle “ghost” plants a new gesture every few seconds when no one is drawing.
The system's classification only appeared in a corner readout.Each reading—ORBIT, BRANCH, DRIFT, PULSE—now fades in where the gesture was drawn.
The “selected experiments” were static placeholder graphics.Replaced with three live gardens running identical input at 20%, 62% and 92% agency.
Low agency still let orbits wander off.Orbit particles are now tethered to their origin in proportion to (1 − agency), so “Echo” really echoes.
Responses were sparse and short-lived.More seeds per gesture, a higher particle ceiling, and branching that grows more often at high agency.
Keyboard users could only plant one kind of seed.O, B, D and P plant each of the four behaviours; the animation pauses when off-screen.
05

Reflection

What changed. The earliest version produced random particles. Classifying gesture qualities gave each continuation a readable relationship to its source.

What I learned. More autonomy does not automatically create more interest. The strongest behaviour preserves a trace of the visitor's decision while resisting perfect obedience. Emergence needs constraints.

Limitation. This is a procedural system, not a trained machine-learning model. Its interpretations are authored rules, disclosed openly rather than presented as mysterious intelligence. That makes it a useful counterpart to Restitch, where a real model reads the visitor's hand.

What I decided

  • QuestionHow much of a gesture remains yours once a system continues it.
  • Interaction modelDraw, release, observe; one agency slider as the only control over authorship.
  • Gesture categoriesOrbit, branch, drift and pulse, and what each should mean.
  • Visual language & pacingNight field, four hues, slow trails, optional sound.
