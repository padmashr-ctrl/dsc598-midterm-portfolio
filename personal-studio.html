A warm minimalist sanctuary for reading and creative work, specified as data so it could be rebuilt in three dimensions.

01

The brief

A room that holds two very different kinds of time: four to six hours of focused creative work, and two to three hours of slow, reflective reading.

The studio had to showcase a personal collection without clutter, maximise natural light without losing warmth, and still feel like a sanctuary rather than an office. Three zones—seating, display and work—stay distinct but visually connected through one controlled system of colour, material and light.

05

Questions from critique

Two questions classmates asked that the first version of this page never answered.

What makes it a sanctuary rather than just a functional workspace?Classmate question

The split is spatial and timed. The work zone takes the window and the brightest light for the long, focused hours. The reading zone turns away from it, toward the burgundy wall, a single chair and a lamp: one person, one book, nothing to manage. The shelving sits between them as a threshold, so moving from one to the other means passing the things I care about.

What makes it a space of the future rather than one we could build today?Classmate question

Very little of the furniture is futuristic, and that was deliberate. What is new is the method: the room exists first as a precise specification—RGB values, a colour temperature, named furniture, three zones—that any generator or 3D tool can rebuild consistently. The future it points to is a designer who writes rooms as data and checks every render against them.

06

Reflection

What worked. RGB values, precise furniture names and consistent lighting language held the palette and program together across every perspective.

What I learned. Emotional descriptors shaped the aesthetic more than style names did. “Sanctuary” was more useful than “modern.” Specificity eliminates ambiguity.

Limitations. The AI reference views are low resolution, and the walkthrough is assembled from stills rather than a continuous camera path through the Blender scene. A next version would render the walkthrough directly in Blender.

AI use. An AI image generator produced the reference views from my prompts. I wrote the brief, the zoning and the colour system, revised the prompts, and built the final render in Blender.
