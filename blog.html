Entry 04 · Shared agency

Interaction needs a consequence.

The strongest interaction is not the one with the most controls. It is the one where a small gesture leaves a readable trace.

In Restitch, a stitch does not simply return when the visitor releases it. It comes back from another image. That single rule turns a visual effect into the concept: every touch edits the archive. I learned to judge an interaction by what changes after the hand has gone.

Entry 03 · Testing

Confidence is part of the interface.

A wrong answer does not create hallucination by itself. The experience comes from how calmly the system presents it.

Playtesting showed that people trusted clear language even after they noticed the archive changing. The final version therefore moves from loud correction to quiet omission, then asks the player to audit the evidence. The game became less about guessing correctly and more about noticing what certainty does to memory.

Entry 02 · Art direction

A mood becomes useful when it can be checked.

“Quiet and anxious” was not a prompt. One heart, 34% across and 62% down, was.

The album-cover project taught me to translate feeling into observable choices: scale, placement, colour and material. Once the target was measurable, each generation could be evaluated instead of merely liked. AI became easier to direct when I stopped treating it as a mind reader.

Entry 01 · Working method

Define. Measure. Refine. Audit.

Ten weeks of experiments became one repeatable way of working with generative systems.

I now begin by defining the intended experience, measuring the choices that matter, refining one variable at a time and auditing what the system changed without permission. The last step is the most important: good output is not the same as accountable process.
