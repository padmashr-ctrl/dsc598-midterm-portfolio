01

Concept

Every image I made this semester with AI is, in some sense, a reconstruction—assembled by a model from everything it has seen. Restitch makes that visible and hands the needle to the viewer.

The work begins as my album cover, rebuilt from thousands of tiny cross-stitches on dark linen. Reach toward the screen and pinch, and the stitches come loose, trailing red thread back to where they belong. Let go and they are sewn back in—but not as they were. Each returning stitch comes from the next memory in the archive: the studio I specified as data, then the room from Prove You Remember, then the heart again.

Nothing is deleted and nothing is invented; every stitch is a pixel from an image I made. Yet after a few minutes of touching, the picture becomes a patchwork that never existed: a heart with a bookshelf in it, a room with a loose red thread. It is a hallucination produced entirely by attention—by where people chose to put their hands.

The piece continues the questions behind the game. There, a system rewrote memory through the player's choices. Here, the body does it directly. The interface is the oldest one I know for careful making—a needle and thread—placed in front of the newest: a neural network watching your fingers.

02

How it works

Three rules, applied to every stitch, sixty times a second.

01 · Sample

Image → stitches

Each source image is fitted to the screen and sampled on a grid. Every grid cell becomes one X-shaped stitch carrying the colour of its pixel, for all three images at once.

02 · Sense

Hand → force

The camera feed goes to a hand-landmark model in the browser. An open hand brushes stitches aside; a thumb-to-index pinch pulls them loose. A pointer or touch does the same.

03 · Return

Loose → home

Loose stitches drift, then a spring draws them back along their thread. Arriving home, each stitch advances to the next image in the archive.

04 · Idle

The ghost hand

When no one is present, an invisible hand keeps wandering and occasionally pulls a thread, so the work keeps rewriting itself in an empty room.

03

Design decisions

Why cross-stitch?Material

The stitch was already my visual language from the album cover. It is also a grid—a hand-made pixel—so it lets an AI image be rendered in a craft vocabulary without hiding what it is.

Why does the stitch come back as a different image?Behaviour

A plain undo would make the touch meaningless. Having each stitch return from the next memory makes every gesture leave a trace, and it echoes how generative systems “repair” a gap with something plausible rather than something true.

Why a hand, not just a mouse?Interaction

Pinching the air to pull a thread is close to the real gesture of sewing, and it puts the body back into a process that is usually just clicks. The mouse fallback keeps the work open to everyone, including people who do not want a camera on.

Why keep the model on the device?Ethics

A piece about who gets to rewrite an image should not quietly send your image elsewhere. The hand model runs inside the browser; no frames are recorded or uploaded, and the camera is only requested when you press the button.

04

Reflection

What worked. Treating all three images as layers of the same grid made the “wrong” restitching possible with almost no extra machinery, and the red return-threads make the invisible logic readable without any instructions.

Limitations. Hand tracking needs decent light and a camera, and pinch detection is a simple distance threshold, so it can misfire with fast movement. Stitch density is lowered on small screens to keep the animation smooth. The work uses existing images rather than generating new ones live; a next version could ask a generative model to stitch in a fourth, never-made memory from what visitors pulled.

Testing. I should still watch three or four people use it without instructions and note how long they take to discover the pinch.

05

AI use & credits

What the tools did, and what I decided.

What AI did

  • MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (Google)A pre-trained machine-learning model that finds 21 points on each hand in the camera image, in real time, in the browser. Apache 2.0 licence.
  • Claude Code (Anthropic)Wrote the simulation, rendering and hand-tracking code from my concept and behaviour notes.
  • Source imagesThe album cover was generated with Canva AI and the studio from an AI reference rendered in Blender, both in earlier projects. The room photograph is from Unsplash.

What I decided

  • ConceptThe archive that repairs with the wrong memory; the needle as the interface.
  • Material & colourCross-stitch on dark linen; one red for every thread.
  • BehaviourBrush vs. pinch, the slow return, the order of the three memories, the idle ghost hand.
  • EthicsOn-device tracking, camera only on request, a full pointer alternative.
